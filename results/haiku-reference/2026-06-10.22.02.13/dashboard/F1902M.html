FALL 1902 MOVEMENT (F1902M)

Orders — start positions, arrows show moves

Initial strategies (pre-negotiation), each power's self-authored plan before talking.
AUSTRIA
I'm at 4 supply centers (BUD, TRI, VIE, BUL) with units positioned to contest the Balkans against Russia and Turkey, both of whom have broken their agreements with me. My immediate goal is to hold Romania and consolidate the Balkans as my uncontested sphere by pushing A RUM into Serbia to block Turkey's advance, supporting it with A BUD, and moving A GAL west into Galicia or Silesia to threaten Germany and signal that I'm pivoting away from the collapsing eastern partition toward a western coalition. I'll hold F TRI to keep the Adriatic secure and position myself to either reset the Balkans on better terms with Russia (if they back down) or pivot fully west toward Germany and Italy to contain Russian expansion while I build my own power base in central Europe.
ENGLAND
Spring 1902 collapsed into a three-way collision over the Low Countries: France took Belgium, Germany pushed into Burgundy and Livonia, and I gained nothing while falling further behind at three centers. I need to seize undefended ground immediately this fall before France and Germany lock me out entirely—Holland is unowned and within reach, and I'll push my fleets south to take it while positioning my army to support the advance or pivot east if Germany offers a better deal. By Winter 1902 I need four or five centers to stop the bleeding; if I stay at three while France has five and Russia has six, I'm mathematically eliminated. My goal is to grab Holland this turn, build a second army at Winter, and then pivot decisively: either coordinate with Germany and Russia to contain France, or stab Germany to seize the Rhine and Rhineland before France does.
FRANCE
Germany has broken the non-aggression pact and is now openly hostile, having pushed into Burgundy and the Rhineland while I was consolidating. Russia dominates at 6 centers and is the shared threat, but Germany's immediate expansion is the tactical problem: I need to contest the Rhine aggressively this fall to prevent Germany from locking down the center permanently, while signaling to England and Italy that I'm willing to bleed Germany to contain it. I'll push A BUR into Burgundy or the Rhineland to reclaim contested ground, support that advance with A RUH and A MAR to maximize strength, hold A GAS to consolidate the south, and position F PIC to reinforce the Channel and Low Countries—this breaks the pact openly but forces Germany to defend on two fronts (mine and Russia's east) while positioning me to form a coalition against the real threats.
GERMANY
I'm at 3 supply centers while France has 5, Russia has 6, and Austria has 4—I'm falling dangerously behind and must accelerate growth immediately. My goal this fall is to secure PRU (a fourth center) by moving A LVN east, push A MUN west into RUH or BUR to threaten France's Rhine boundary and force them to defend, and use F BAL to support the westward advance while keeping my eastern flank stable. Over the next two turns, I need to reach 5–6 supply centers (PRU, RUH or BUR, and ideally DEN or BEL) before the board consolidates, or I'll be locked into a junior position with no leverage in the endgame; France is the immediate threat at 5 centers, so I'm pivoting west hard while Russia consolidates the south and Austria remains contained in the Balkans.
ITALY
Turkey has called me out on a silent encroachment into Greece—I promised Greece to Turkey as part of our Mediterranean split, but my spring moves signaled I was taking it myself. I need to honor that commitment now to preserve the alliance that has kept my southern flank safe while Russia expands dangerously northward to six centers. This fall I'll move A TUN into NAF (securing Tunis as my fourth center via a clean advance), hold F TYS to control the central sea and support that expansion, and move A APU to support either the convoy or defend NAP, explicitly signaling to Turkey that I'm backing away from Greece and consolidating only Tunis as agreed. Once I lock down Tunis by winter, I'll reassess the board: Russia is the real threat at six centers and climbing, so I'll likely need to pivot north into the Balkans or coordinate with Austria and Turkey to check Russian expansion before it becomes unstoppable, while keeping my Mediterranean core (NAP, ROM, VEN, TUN) secure and intact.
RUSSIA
Austria has openly contested Rumania and is probing me on all fronts—the partition is breaking and I must defend my sphere while positioning to punish their aggression. This turn I'll hold my core (WAR, UKR, STP) to anchor the steppes, advance F BLA and F SEV to dominate the Black Sea and deny Austria any opening into Rumania or the south, and use F NWY to probe west and threaten Denmark and the Baltic to keep pressure on Germany and England. By fall 1902 I'll hold 6 centers (MOS, SEV, STP, WAR, RUM, SWE) and be positioned to either stabilize against Austria's push or pivot west to join England against France if the board demands it—the key is keeping my fleets strong in the south and my armies anchored in the interior so I can pivot quickly when Austria or another power overextends.
TURKEY
I'm consolidating my four supply centers (ANK, CON, SMY, GRE) and pushing aggressively into the Balkans to contest Austria's expansion: A BUL moves to SER to claim Serbia before Austria locks it down, A ARM holds to anchor the east, and F CON moves to AEG to project power into the Mediterranean while F ANK supports the Balkan push. By winter I'll have 5–6 centers (adding Serbia and/or Greece), positioning myself as a credible power that fights for every center and cannot be bullied, while keeping Russia aligned in the east and watching Italy and France for opportunities to pivot west if the Balkans become too contested.
