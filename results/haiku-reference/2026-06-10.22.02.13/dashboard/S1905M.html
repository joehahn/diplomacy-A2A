SPRING 1905 MOVEMENT (S1905M)

Orders — start positions, arrows show moves

Initial strategies (pre-negotiation), each power's self-authored plan before talking.
AUSTRIA
Russia dominates at 7 centers while I'm at 4, and the western coalition has fractured—France is isolated at 5, Germany collapsed to 1, and Italy consolidated to 5. I must reset hard this spring: A RUM pushes into UKR to threaten Livonia and force Russia to split defenses, A GAL moves into SIL to contest their western anchor directly, A BUD supports A RUM's advance, and F TRI holds to secure the Adriatic. By winter I'll consolidate 5–6 centers (BUD, TRI, SER, RUM, and one more if I can peel away an undefended center or reset with Italy/Turkey), and I'll use the winter window to rebuild a southern coalition with Italy and Turkey against Russia's dominance, or pivot west into the Balkans to anchor a new defensive perimeter if Russia breaks south toward my core.
ENGLAND
Russia is now at 7 centers and pulling away as the board leader; I'm at 6 and need to reach 8 this year to stay competitive. My immediate goal is to seize undefended ground in the north (Sweden or Norway) by pushing F HEL into the Baltic and positioning my northern army to support a sweep through Scandinavia, while keeping my Rhine position (A BEL) anchored defensively to prevent Germany or France from exploiting the west. By Fall 1905, I want to control 8 centers (adding SWE and NWY to my current six) and position for a coordinated two-front assault on Russia with France in Winter 1905 or Spring 1906, since Russia's runaway lead now threatens everyone and demands a coalition response.
FRANCE
Russia has consolidated to 7 centers and is now the dominant threat; Austria at 4 and Italy at 5 remain secondary. Germany collapsed to 1 center (KIE only) after losing LVN and RUH, so the western front is effectively won—I can now pivot hard east with England and Austria to bleed Russia before it reaches 10+ and becomes unstoppable. This spring I'll advance A VIE into BOH to threaten Silesia and force Russia to defend the north, push A BUR toward MUN to consolidate the Rhine and free growth room, hold A PAR and A GAS to anchor the west, and position F PIC to support either a Mediterranean push with Italy or reinforce the Channel if needed—the goal is to reach 6–7 centers by Fall 1905 while coordinating with England and Austria to pin Russia hard on multiple fronts and prevent it from reaching 10.
GERMANY
I'm at a critical juncture: I hold only KIE (1 center) after losing LVN to Russia's WAR army in Fall 1904, while Russia dominates at 7 centers, England holds 6, France holds 5, and Austria holds 4. My only viable path forward is to rebuild my position in the north and east before the board locks irreversibly. This spring I'll move F BAL north to secure a foothold (either into LVN to reclaim lost ground or into SWE to threaten Denmark), probe Austria and England on containing Russia's expansion, and signal that I'm pivoting east to bleed Russia rather than west against the Rhine coalition—if I can convince Austria to push Russia hard from the Balkans while I pressure from the north, I can stabilize at 2–3 centers and position myself for growth next turn; if Russia consolidates unchecked, I'm eliminated within two turns, so my only leverage is offering Austria a coordinated two-front assault on Russia's western flank.
ITALY
I'm entering Spring 1905 with five supply centers (NAP, ROM, VEN, TUN, MUN) and five units positioned to consolidate toward six centers this spring by pushing A MUN east into either SIL or BER to threaten Russia's core, while using F NAP and F TYS to keep the Mediterranean secure and prepare for a potential pivot north if Russia's dominance at seven centers (after gaining BER and SWE in Fall 1904) forces a coordinated multi-power containment. My immediate goal is to reach six centers by winter and signal to Austria and Turkey that Russia's unchecked growth toward nine is now the shared existential threat—if Russia pivots south or west toward any of us, we coordinate immediately to contain it before the endgame reshapes the board entirely.
RUSSIA
I'm now at 7 supply centers (MOS, SEV, STP, WAR, SWE, NWY, BER) and have momentum after claiming Berlin in Fall 1904, but Austria (4), France (5), and England (6) are still competitive and the board is consolidating. My immediate goal is to push A BER west into Silesia to anchor my western flank decisively, advance A WAR and A PRU to support that push and threaten Austria's Galicia, hold A STP and A MOS to anchor my rear and support the western advance, and maintain F BLA and F SEV dominance in the Black Sea to lock Austria out of the south. Within two turns I aim to reach 9–10 supply centers (Silesia, Livonia, and one more from Austria or Germany), force Austria and Germany to defend their cores instead of probing me, and position myself to either stabilize the east or pivot west to contain France if England falters—this is my window to break out before the endgame locks around the western powers.
TURKEY
Russia dominates at seven centers after winter and has anchored Berlin and Prussia—it is now the clear board leader and my primary long-term threat, especially since Austria has pivoted hard north against it and Italy remains focused on consolidating the west. This spring I will hold my core (A BUL, A SYR, F ANK, F SMY, F EAS) defensively while I probe Austria and Italy to confirm whether they are genuinely committed to containing Russia together, or whether one of them is already pivoting toward accommodation with the leader; by fall 1905 I need to know if a coordinated three-power counter-alliance against Russia is viable, or if I must shift to independent expansion in the Mediterranean and Balkans. If Austria and Italy hold firm against Russia this turn, I'll move into the attack next fall; if either wavers, I'll pivot immediately to exploit the opening and expand before Russia consolidates further.

ENGLAND
Russia has now declared that Sweden and Norway are in its sphere and non-aggression doesn't cover unowned centers—a clear signal it will seize both this spring, which would push it to 9 centers and make it nearly unstoppable. I need to pivot immediately: I'll move F HEL into the Baltic to contest the north, push A BEL east into RUH to join France's pressure on the center, and hold my island units defensively to anchor my six centers while I reposition for a coordinated two-front assault on Russia with France and Austria. By Fall 1905 I aim to reach 7-8 centers by taking contested ground in the center and north, and by Spring 1906 I'll be ready to execute a full coalition squeeze on Russia's overextended position.
FRANCE
Russia at 7 centers is now the dominant threat and must be bled hard on multiple fronts this spring—Austria is pivoting north from RUM toward UKR, Italy is pushing MUN into SIL, England is consolidating Scandinavia, and Turkey stands ready to press from the south. I'll commit A VIE into BOH to threaten Silesia and force Russia to defend the north, A BUR into MUN to consolidate the Rhine and support Italy's pressure, A PAR and A GAS holding defensively to anchor my core, and F PIC holding to reinforce the Channel—this executes the eastern coalition strategy while keeping my western flank secure against Germany's unpredictable pivot, and positions me to reach 6–7 centers by Fall 1905 if Russia fractures under coordinated multi-front pressure.
GERMANY
I'm at a critical moment: holding only KIE while Russia dominates at 7 centers and the board is consolidating around stronger powers. My negotiation with Austria yielded no commitment, England is neutral and pivoting north, and Russia is offering me a path west against France—but pivoting west into a France-England coalition that already warned me away is suicide. My only viable play is to move F BAL west into the North Sea to probe for undefended centers (LVP is weakly held, and if England is truly pivoting north, the Low Countries may open), signal that I'm respecting Russia's dominance and Austria's indifference by not pushing east, and keep my options open for next turn when the board's power distribution becomes clearer—if France or England stumbles, I'll be positioned to exploit it; if they consolidate further, I'll have at least preserved my last center and avoided a pointless two-front war that guarantees my elimination.
ITALY
I'm moving A MUN into SIL to contest Russia's core directly while Austria simultaneously pushes from the Balkans, forcing Russia to split its defenses across multiple fronts; my fleets (F NAP, F TYS) will hold defensively in the Mediterranean to maintain my position and stay ready to pivot if Russia breaks west or Austria falters, and A TUN will hold to keep my Tunis center secure. This honors my coordination with Austria against the board-dominating threat while preserving my Mediterranean alliance with Turkey and keeping my options open to shift east if the Balkan pressure fails to contain Russia's growth.
RUSSIA
I'm at 7 supply centers and have momentum after Berlin, but Austria, France, and England remain competitive. My strategy is to push A BER into Silesia to anchor my western flank decisively, advance A PRU and A WAR to support that push and threaten Austria's core, hold A STP and A MOS to anchor my rear, and maintain Black Sea dominance with F BLA and F SEV to lock Austria out of the south—this reaches 8–9 supply centers by fall and forces Austria and Germany to defend instead of probing me. Germany has pivoted west against France (no eastern threat), Austria is consolidating the Balkans (no immediate northern aggression), and England holds the north (non-aggression confirmed on our shared frontier), so I have a clear window to break out before the endgame locks around the western powers.
TURKEY
Russia's dominance at seven centers and the coordinated pressure from Austria, England, France, and Italy make this the critical moment to join the counter-alliance decisively. I'll move A BUL north into RUM to support Austria's attack on Russia's southern flank, hold A SYR defensively to secure my eastern core, and position my three fleets (F ANK, F SMY, F EAS) to support the Balkan pressure and keep my supply centers locked down—this commits me fully to the anti-Russia coalition while preserving my ability to expand into the Mediterranean once Russia fractures under the weight of simultaneous attacks from four directions. If Austria, England, France, and Italy maintain pressure through fall 1905, Russia will splinter and I can consolidate my gains; if any coalition member wavers or pivots toward accommodation, I'll shift immediately to independent expansion in the Mediterranean and Balkans before the board reshuffles again.
