SPRING 1909 MOVEMENT (S1909M)

Orders — start positions, arrows show moves

Initial strategies (pre-negotiation), each power's self-authored plan before talking.
AUSTRIA
The board has fractured into regional powers: Russia locked at 4 centers in the north, France consolidating at 6 in the center, Italy and Turkey both at 6 in the south and east, England dominant at 7 in the west, and me at 4 in the Balkans. My strategy is to consolidate the Balkans-Mediterranean axis this spring by moving A UKR into RUM to seize it before Turkey can expand further, supporting that attack with A VIE into GAL to threaten Russia's western flank simultaneously, and holding F TRI defensively to anchor against Italy's potential betrayal or to pivot south if Italy commits to a genuine partition. By fall I'll have 5–6 centers (BUD, TRI, SER, RUM, GAL, and possibly one more from Italy's collapse), positioned to either execute a three-power Balkans-east consolidation with Turkey if they pivot north again, or reset south with Italy and France if the Mediterranean fractures and a new coalition emerges to bleed the leader.
ENGLAND
The board has stalled: I hold 7 centers, Italy and Turkey both hold 6, France holds 6, Austria holds 4, Russia holds 4, Germany holds 2. France's southern squeeze on Italy failed completely last fall, and the Mediterranean powers remain locked while I anchor the west defensively. This spring I need to break the stalemate decisively—I'll pivot my southern army (A BUR) and fleet resources toward Italy and the Mediterranean directly, coordinate with France and Austria on a hard three-power squeeze to fracture Italy down to 5 centers by fall, and position myself to claim VEN or APU by winter 1909, giving me 8 centers and the momentum to reach 12+ by 1910 before Turkey locks the board. If Italy fractures, Austria pivots north into the Balkans and I pivot south with my fleets to contest the Mediterranean; if Italy holds firm, I'll reset and accept a draw rather than bleed myself against a locked position.
FRANCE
The board is locked: England at 7, Italy and Turkey at 6 each, Russia at 4, me at 6, Austria at 3, Germany at 2. My repeated southern squeeze commitments have failed—Russia bounced twice into SEV, Italy holds firm, and the Mediterranean coalition remains intact. I'm abandoning growth-focused diplomacy and pivoting to pure defensive consolidation: A BER, A MUN, A SIL, A PAR, and A GAS all anchor the Rhine and center to signal I'm no longer betting on expansion, while F PIC and F MAR hold the coasts. This credible defensive posture keeps Germany and Austria from probing west and positions me to survive until a genuine reset opportunity emerges—either Austria or Russia finally pressures the actual leaders (England, Italy, Turkey), or the board fractures and I'm positioned to exploit the chaos with my 6 intact centers.
GERMANY
I'm at 2 centers (KIE, NWY) facing a board where England dominates at 7, France holds 6, and Russia anchors 4—my survival depends on breaking into the east or west immediately. This spring I'll move F KIE to BAL to position for a Baltic breakthrough (supporting Austria into RUM or pressing toward SEV myself next turn) and move F NWY toward STP/NC to threaten Russia's northern flank and signal to Austria and Turkey that I'm ready to coordinate a three-way pincer if they commit concrete support. If Austria moves A GAL or A UKR toward RUM this turn, I have leverage to support their attack next spring and both of us gain; if Austria holds defensively, I'll reassess whether to pivot west and probe for a fracture in the England-France coalition or collapse into a spoiler role bleeding Russia while the board consolidates around me.
ITALY
France remains the central threat at 6 centers, and I need to break the stalemate before England (7 centers) consolidates further dominance. This spring I'll execute a coordinated two-front pressure: A TUN convoys north via the Ionian to contest the center and force France to defend MUN/BER from multiple angles, while F ROM and F TUS maintain Mediterranean control and prepare to support a northern push into Tyrrhenian and Ionian waters. I'll probe Austria and Russia on whether they're ready to coordinate containment of France's overextension, signal Turkey that the Mediterranean pact holds firm, and position myself to reclaim BER or seize undefended Balkan centers by fall 1909 if France falters under coordinated pressure from north and south.
RUSSIA
I'm at 4 centers facing slow strangulation while England dominates at 7, France holds 6, and Turkey consolidates at 6—my only path forward is to break Turkey's lock on Sevastopol this spring with F RUM into SEV, anchor my core defensively with A WAR and A LVN supporting the chain, and pivot A GAL west toward contested ground (BOH or SIL) to signal availability for a three-power squeeze on the Mediterranean powers before one runs away with the game. If I reach 6 centers by winter and France stabilizes at 6-7, we can coordinate next spring to contain Italy and Turkey while Germany remains a weak eastern spoiler; if Turkey holds SEV again, I'm finished by fall 1909 and must accept elimination or a desperate stab at Austria.
TURKEY
The board has locked into a defensive stalemate across the east and Balkans: Austria controls 4 centers, Italy 4, Russia 4, and I control 6—the northern powers (England 7, France 6, Germany 2) are grinding each other down while I sit secure. My goal this spring is to hold the eastern anchor (A SEV and A SYR defensive), keep my fleets locked in the straits and eastern Mediterranean (F BLA, F AEG, F BUL/SC, F ION), and watch for the first fracture in the north—once England, France, or Germany stumbles badly enough to expose a center, I'll pivot west next fall and press into the chaos before any rival can consolidate. For now, pure defense and patience: every turn the northern powers bleed is a turn I grow relatively stronger without spending force.

AUSTRIA
The dialogue reveals a fractured board where every power is hedging: England wants me to bleed Italy/Turkey but won't commit hard; France abandoned the south and wants me to hold; Germany wants me to press Russia but won't support without confirmation; Russia is offering a two-front squeeze against Turkey; Italy claims non-aggression but watches for pivots; Turkey demands consolidation; and I'm at only 4 centers while surrounded by 6-7 center powers. I'll hold defensively this spring—A UKR holds in UKR, A VIE holds in VIE, F TRI holds in TRI—to preserve my core, signal stability to Turkey and Russia, and avoid overextending into a losing fight while the board reshuffles around me. By fall I'll have clarity on whether Russia and Turkey fracture under pressure from the north and east, and I can pivot aggressively into the Balkans once the Mediterranean lock breaks and a clearer path to growth emerges.
ENGLAND
France has signaled defensive consolidation on the Rhine and center, Austria is holding defensively in the Balkans, and Germany is pivoting east toward Russia—the three-power squeeze on Italy is dead, and the board remains locked at 6-7 centers across the Mediterranean powers. I'll hold my core defensively (A EDI, A YOR, F LVP, F LON, F HOL, F SWE all hold), move A BUR south to support France's center (supporting into MUN to keep pressure on Italy without overcommitting), and signal to France and Austria that I'm consolidating at 7 while watching for any power to fracture or break the stalemate. If the board remains frozen through fall 1909, I'll accept a draw; if Italy or Turkey weakens, I'll pivot south aggressively to claim the Mediterranean before winter 1910.
FRANCE
Russia's proposal to pivot A GAL west into BOH is a direct escalation into the center that signals a three-power squeeze on England—but it also exposes Russia's core to Austria and commits me to a defensive anchor at SIL and MUN while Russia gambles on the center. Italy's confirmation of mutual survival and England's consolidation at 7 centers with no western escalation signal the board is locked defensively for now, which means holding the Rhine firm and keeping my options open is safer than betting on a three-power center squeeze that requires Russia to hold its own east while I anchor the Rhine. I'll consolidate defensively this spring—A BER, A MUN, A SIL, A PAR, and A GAS all hold their ground to anchor the Rhine and center, while F PIC and F MAR hold the coasts—signaling to all powers that I'm not escalating and positioning myself to survive until a genuine reset opportunity emerges, whether that's Austria finally cracking Turkey or the board fracturing under England's consolidation pressure.
GERMANY
Russia's refusal to offer growth and Austria's defensive hold have left me isolated in an eastern pivot—I cannot bleed Russia effectively alone while England consolidates the west and France anchors 6 centers. I'm pivoting back to a western probe: F KIE moves to BAL to threaten Scandinavia and keep England honest on our non-aggression pact, and F NWY moves to NTH to test whether England's promise of defensive anchoring holds or whether I can exploit a fracture in the western coalition by pressing toward HOL or BEL next turn. If England honors the pact and holds defensively, I'll reassess toward a spoiler role bleeding Russia; if England contests NTH or BEL opens, I'll have a western growth path that keeps me alive through fall 1909.
ITALY
Russia has not committed to coordinating against France this spring, and England is actively recruiting me south against Turkey while Austria pressures the Balkans—the board is fragmenting and I cannot afford to wait on Russia's answer. I'll pivot to the more certain gain: anchor my Mediterranean core defensively (A NAP holds, F ROM and F TUS secure the Tyrrhenian), reposition A TUN south to NAF to seize the unowned supply center and signal England I'm willing to bleed Turkey, and honor Turkey's pact by not attacking them directly this turn while positioning myself to capitalize on the chaos when Austria and England press them from multiple sides. This keeps my options open, secures a fourth supply center by winter, and lets me read whether Russia pivots west or south before committing to a longer alliance next spring.
RUSSIA
I'm pivoting west this spring to contest the center and signal availability for a three-power squeeze on England: A GAL moves into BOH to pressure France's overextended position, A LVN supports that move to add strength, A PRU holds defensively as my core anchor, and F RUM holds in place to keep my southern flank stable against Turkey and Austria while I reposition my weight northward. This commits me to the France alliance against England's seven centers, abandons the Turkey squeeze as unwinnable, and sets up next spring's coordinated pressure on the board leader if France holds SIL and MUN defensively as promised.
TURKEY
The board remains locked defensively across the east and straits, with Austria and Italy consolidating south while England, France, Germany, and Russia grind each other down in the north. Germany's pincer offer is tempting but premature—pressing SEV or BUL now while Russia still holds 4 centers and Austria can pivot anytime would expose my eastern anchor without guarantee of gain, so I'm holding my defensive posture this spring (A SEV and A SYR hold, fleets F BLA, F AEG, F BUL/SC, F ION anchor the straits and eastern Mediterranean) and watching for the first real fracture in the north before I pivot west next fall. My plan is to consolidate through winter 1909, preserve my 6 centers and rebuild slots, and move aggressively into the chaos once England, France, or Germany collapses badly enough to expose undefended centers in the center of the board.
