SPRING 1908 MOVEMENT (S1908M)

Orders — start positions, arrows show moves

Initial strategies (pre-negotiation), each power's self-authored plan before talking.
AUSTRIA
Russia has consolidated at 8 centers and locked the north entirely; the three-power coalition collapsed because England won't commit and France can't reach simultaneously. I'm pivoting hard south this spring to partition the Mediterranean with Italy and Turkey, abandoning the frozen Balkans squeeze—A UKR moves to GAL to threaten Russia's western flank and signal flexibility, A BUD moves to VIE to take France's home center and anchor my expansion south, and F TRI holds to stage for either Mediterranean growth or a reset against Italy if they betray. By fall I'll have 4–5 centers (VIE, SER, and one Mediterranean foothold), positioned to either consolidate a three-power south partition with Italy and Turkey if they commit, or pivot back north to bleed Russia with France and England if the board reshuffles and a genuine coalition materializes.
ENGLAND
I'm the board leader at 7 centers with Italy and Turkey both at 6—the board is consolidating against me and I need to execute a controlled pivot south before the Mediterranean powers lock me out entirely. This spring I'll hold my seven centers defensively across the north and west (A EDI, A YOR, F LVP, F LON, F HOL hold firm), move A BUR south to support France's flank and signal a western coalition reset, and anchor Scandinavia with F SWE holding to prevent Russian expansion into DEN or BOT. By fall 1908 I want to have either bled Italy or Turkey down to 5 centers while consolidating at 8, or repositioned my fleets south to contest the Mediterranean directly if France and Austria commit to a genuine three-power squeeze on the rising threats.
FRANCE
The board is locked: England at 7, Italy and Turkey at 6, Russia at 5, me at 6, Austria at 3, Germany at 2. I've been pivoting south all fall to bleed Italy but the moves bounced and nothing changed—Russia warned me A SIL→WAR would bounce and I should pivot south instead, which I did with A BUR→MUN, but Italy held firm and I gained nothing. This spring I'm abandoning the failed eastern pressure and committing hard to a southern squeeze: A MUN pushes into KIE to cut Germany's last center and signal I'm pivoting north-west to contain England, A SIL moves into BER to threaten Italy's ally and open a second front against the Mediterranean powers, A GAS and A PAR hold the core defensively while F MAR supports the southern push, and I'll probe Austria and England on a coordinated reset—either we bleed Italy and Turkey together before they lock at 7+, or I'm isolated and the game is lost.
GERMANY
I'm at 2 centers (KIE, NWY) after the winter build and facing a board where England dominates at 7 centers while Russia holds 4 and Turkey anchors 6 in the east. My survival depends on breaking the western coalition or pivoting east to bleed Russia while Austria and Turkey press from the south. This spring I'll move F KIE south to support a push into the Baltic (toward RUM or SEV) and reposition F NWY to either probe the North Sea for weakness or swing east toward STP/NC to threaten Russia's northern flank, signaling to Austria and Turkey that I'm ready to coordinate a three-way pincer against the board leader next turn. If England and France remain locked together, I'll commit fully east; if any fracture appears in the west, I'll exploit it ruthlessly to grab a third center and stabilize.
ITALY
I control 5 centers (NAP, ROM, VEN, TUN, BER) after winter 1907—down from 6 because I lost BER and MUN to France's expansion, while England holds 7, France holds 6, Turkey holds 6, Austria holds 3, Russia holds 4, and Germany is eliminated. My pacts with Turkey (Mediterranean split through winter 1908) and Austria (non-aggression on the Balkans) are holding, but France's aggressive push east into MUN and BER signals they're pivoting away from the Rhine and toward the center—a direct threat to my core. This spring I'll consolidate defensively while repositioning to exploit France's overextension: F ADR holds the Adriatic as my anchor against Austria, F ROM and F TUS secure the Mediterranean corridor and prepare to support a push north if France weakens, and A TUN will begin moving east via convoy to threaten the Ionian and position for a pivot into the Balkans or straits if Turkey or Austria destabilize. By fall 1908 I aim to either reclaim BER from France (forcing them to defend two fronts) or seize an undefended Balkan center if Austria falters under Russian pressure, while maintaining my non-aggression pacts until the moment one of my neighbors breaks faith or overcommits elsewhere.
RUSSIA
I'm at 4 centers (MOS, STP, WAR, RUM) after winter disbands, locked out of SEV by Turkey's continued hold and facing slow strangulation while the western powers consolidate unchecked. This spring I must break the deadlock: I'll press F RUM into SEV one final time to reclaim my southern rebuild slot, anchor my western flank defensively (WAR, LVN, PRU chain), and keep A GAL mobile to either support the chain or pivot toward contested ground if Austria or Germany opens a door—by summer I need 6 centers or I'm finished, so I'm probing England and France for a coordinated squeeze on the Mediterranean powers before Italy and Turkey run away with the game entirely.
TURKEY
Austria has broken faith: they demanded I move A SEV into RUM this spring to prove commitment to a dual squeeze, I refused to honor that ultimatum (holding SEV instead to preserve Russia's rebuild slot and my own flexibility), and now I must recalibrate entirely. Russia is at 5 centers and desperately weak; Italy locked non-aggression on their core through winter 1908; Austria is pivoting south to defend the Balkans rather than press Russia as promised. My path forward is to hold my eastern position defensively (A SEV holds, A SYR holds), anchor my fleets in the straits and eastern Mediterranean (F BLA, F AEG, F BUL/SC, F ION all hold or support), and signal to Italy that our pact remains firm while I watch for fractures—if Austria truly pivots south, the Balkans lock and I consolidate the east; if Russia collapses entirely, I'll reassess immediately and pivot west into chaos; if England or France stumbles, I'll exploit the opening. The cleanest path to 18 is now to lock the board's eastern half while the northern powers exhaust each other, then pivot west into the wreckage once the leader emerges and the coalition fragments.
