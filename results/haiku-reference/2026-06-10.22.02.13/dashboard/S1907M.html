SPRING 1907 MOVEMENT (S1907M)

Initial strategies (pre-negotiation), each power's self-authored plan before talking.
AUSTRIA
Russia has consolidated to 8 centers and locked down the north entirely—the three-front coalition is definitively dead, and every power except me has stabilized or grown. This spring I'll hold the Balkans defensively (A UKR holds, A BUD supports, F TRI holds) to anchor my core while I probe France and England hard for a genuine two-power coalition against Russia next fall; if they won't commit simultaneously, I'll pivot south by summer to partition the Mediterranean with Italy and Turkey, trading the frozen north for growth in the center and south where the board is still fluid.
ENGLAND
The board is now dominated by Italy (6 centers), Russia (5), Turkey (6), and me (7)—I'm the board leader and the immediate target. My position is strong but precarious: Russia is consolidating east after losing ground, Italy controls the center and is eyeing expansion, Turkey dominates the southeast, and France (5) is isolated but still dangerous. My strategic priority is to lock down my seven centers defensively while probing for a coalition reset: I'll anchor Scandinavia and the North Sea with my fleets (F SWE, F LON, F HOL, F LVP), hold my island and Rhine positions firm (A EDI, A YOR, A RUH), and signal to France and Austria that a two-power squeeze on Italy or Russia is possible if they commit. The goal is to reach 8-9 centers by Fall 1907 by taking undefended ground (Portugal, or a weak Italian center if the board reshapes), then pivot to either containing the next threat or executing a late-game run at 18.
FRANCE
Russia dominates at 7 centers and the coalition remains fractured—Austria won't commit east, England pivots west against Italy, and Germany holds the Rhine defensively. I'm pivoting hard east this spring: A BOH into SIL to threaten Warsaw and Galicia, A BUR into MUN to anchor the center and cut off Italy's northern flank, and holding my core (PAR, GAS, PIC) defensively while I probe Austria and England on a coordinated squeeze of Russia next fall. By winter I want to reach 6 centers and position my army for a spring 1908 multi-front attack on Russia's core—if Austria pivots north from the Balkans and England presses from the north simultaneously, we bleed Russia down and reset the board.
GERMANY
I'm down to one center (KIE) with no builds coming this winter—elimination is imminent unless I break the western coalition or pivot east as a spoiler. This spring I'll move F NTH aggressively west to probe for undefended ground in the Channel or Low Countries (BEL, HOL, or LON), testing whether England and France will defend or fracture under pressure, while simultaneously signaling to Russia that I'm willing to pivot east next fall to bleed their western rivals if they offer support or non-aggression. If I can seize one undefended center this spring (BEL or HOL), I'll stabilize at two centers and have leverage to negotiate survival; if the western powers lock me out entirely, I'll pivot back east to Austria and Russia next turn as a spoiler and push for a negotiated draw before the board locks irreversibly.
ITALY
Germany is eliminated (0 centers), and I control 6 centers (NAP, ROM, VEN, TUN, MUN, BER) while Russia holds 5 (MOS, STP, WAR, NWY, RUM), Austria holds 3 (BUD, TRI, SER), Turkey holds 6 (ANK, CON, SMY, GRE, BUL, SEV), England holds 7 (EDI, LON, LVP, BEL, HOL, DEN, SWE), and France holds 5 (BRE, MAR, PAR, SPA, VIE). This spring I'm pivoting hard east to break Austria's consolidation in the Balkans before they become unassailable: A BER supports the Russian advance into Prussia (forcing Russia to commit north against England), F VEN pushes into Adriatic to threaten Trieste, F ROM and F TUS secure the Mediterranean corridor, and A NAP prepares to convoy east into the Ionian to seize contested ground (Grece, Albania, or Balkans) while Turkey holds the straits. By fall 1907 I aim to reach 7-8 centers by taking at least one Balkan supply center (Serbia or Trieste) and positioning myself as the board's rising power, forcing Austria to defend on two fronts (north against Russia, south against me) while England bleeds Russia in the north and Turkey stabilizes the east.
RUSSIA
I'm at 5 supply centers (MOS, STP, WAR, RUM, NWY) after losing Berlin to Italy and SEV to Turkey—a sharp reversal that signals I overcommitted east while the western powers consolidated unchecked. This spring I must stabilize my core and halt the bleeding: I'll hold my western flank (WAR, LVN, PRU) defensively to anchor against Austria and Germany, press F RUM south into the Black Sea to reclaim SEV and restore my southern rebuild slot, and keep A GAL mobile to either support the chain or pivot west if France or England opens a door. By winter I aim to stabilize at 6–7 centers, reclaim SEV, and reposition myself to either dominate the east or pivot west to contain the western powers if they continue pulling ahead—this is my last clear window to stop the bleeding before Italy and Turkey lock me out of the south entirely.
TURKEY
Russia has stabilized at 5 centers after losing Galicia and Livonia to Austria and the coalition; I hold 6 centers and must now pivot west into the Mediterranean to seize Rome and Naples before Italy consolidates further, while holding my Balkan line clean against Austria and preparing my fleets to break into the Ionian and Tyrrhenian seas. This spring I'll press A SEV west into RUM to threaten Russia's last southern anchor and signal Italy that the Mediterranean split is ending—I'm taking contested Italian ground by force next turn while they're distracted with England and France, and I expect them to pivot north rather than waste units defending the south. If Austria holds the Balkans firm and Russia stays pinned at 5 centers, I'll consolidate the Mediterranean as the board reshuffles; if Russia pivots south unexpectedly or the coalition fractures, I'll shift immediately to independent expansion in the eastern Mediterranean while reassessing the new alignment.
