SPRING 1906 MOVEMENT (S1906M)

Orders — start positions, arrows show moves

Initial strategies (pre-negotiation), each power's self-authored plan before talking.
AUSTRIA
Russia dominates at 7 centers while I'm stuck at 3, and the spring coalition with Italy and France collapsed completely—Italy bounced on Silesia, France couldn't anchor, and I've been forced into a defensive consolidation while Russia locked down the north. This spring I need to reset hard: I'll push A UKR north into GAL to threaten Warsaw and Livonia directly, support that advance with A BUD to ensure it lands, and use F TRI to probe south toward Venice or hold the Adriatic as a staging point for a Mediterranean pivot if Italy turns hostile. By fall I'll consolidate 4–5 centers (BUD, TRI, SER, and likely RUM if Turkey holds the line, plus GAL if Russia's defenses crack), and by winter I'll have repositioned to either anchor a new coalition with England and France pressing Russia from three fronts, or pivot south into Italy and Turkey if the north stabilizes and I need to reset the board entirely.
ENGLAND
Russia remains the board leader at 6 centers (tied with Turkey and me), but the coalition against it has fractured—Austria consolidated defensively, France is isolated, and Italy never committed hard enough. I'm pivoting my strategy: instead of chasing a three-front squeeze that won't materialize, I'll consolidate my six centers defensively, reposition my northern units to anchor Scandinavia and the Baltic (F BAL holds the line, A RUH and F HOL defend the Rhine), and secure my build slot at LON for Winter 1906 by keeping A EDI and A YOR firm on the island. My real goal this spring is to probe France and Austria for a new coalition—specifically, can I peel France away from its current isolation by offering coordinated pressure on Italy's flank (Piedmont, Tuscany) while I hold the north against Russia's inevitable spring push? If France and I can reset our partnership and execute a two-power squeeze on Italy while Austria pressures Russia from the Balkans, I can reach 8 centers by Fall 1906 and position for a late-game run at 18. If that fails, I'll hold my six centers defensively and watch for whoever emerges as the next board leader to gang up on.
FRANCE
Russia remains the dominant threat at 7 centers, and the eastern coalition has fractured—Austria is defensive, Italy isolated, England holding the north without committing east. I need to consolidate my 5-center base this spring (holding BOH, BUR, PAR, GAS, and PIC defensively) while signaling to England and Austria that I'm ready to pivot hard east next fall if they commit to a coordinated multi-front squeeze. This buys time to rebuild the coalition or shift strategy if Russia consolidates further, and positions me to either bleed Russia down to 5–6 centers by winter or pivot west against Germany if it resurges. My immediate goal is to reach 6 centers by Fall 1906 while keeping my core secure and my options open for a spring 1907 eastern offensive.
GERMANY
I'm at a critical inflection point: holding only KIE with no builds coming and facing a board where Russia dominates at 7 centers, England controls 6, France and Turkey each hold 5+, and Austria clings to 3. My only viable path to survival is to pivot west aggressively and exploit any fracture in the western coalition before the board locks irreversibly. This spring I'll move F SWE and F KIE west into the North Sea and Baltic to probe for undefended centers—LVP is weakly held by England (who is distracted north), and the Low Countries or Channel may open if France and England clash—while signaling to Russia that I'm respecting the eastern border and pivoting away from Scandinavia entirely. If I can seize one undefended center this turn (LVP or DEN), I'll stabilize at 2 centers and have a fighting chance next winter; if the western powers lock me out, I'll have at least preserved my last center and positioned myself to exploit whichever front fractures first—my long-term survival depends on breaking the France-England-Russia coalition by offering England support against France or vice versa, but I need at least one more center in hand to be a credible partner.
ITALY
Russia has consolidated at 6 centers (down from 7 after losing BER to you and SWE to Germany), and the board has shifted: Austria holds 3 (BUD, TRI, SER), England dominates at 6 (EDI, LON, LVP, BEL, HOL, DEN), France holds 5 (BRE, MAR, PAR, SPA, VIE), Germany is crippled at 2 (KIE, SWE), Turkey holds 6 (ANK, CON, SMY, GRE, BUL, RUM), and you hold 5 (NAP, ROM, VEN, TUN, MUN). The spring coalition against Russia has collapsed entirely—Austria held defensively, France never attacked, England pressed from the north but isolated, and Turkey attacked RUM alone. This spring I'm pivoting hard west to exploit Germany's weakness and England's dominance by consolidating the center: A MUN pushes into KIE to take German territory and threaten the Baltic, F TYS moves into ROM to secure the Mediterranean and prepare for a northern pivot, F NAP supports that attack, A TUN holds Tunis, and F VEN holds to keep the Adriatic secure. By winter 1906 I aim to reach 6 centers (taking KIE from Germany) and position myself as the rising power in the center while England and Russia bleed each other in the north and Austria stabilizes the Balkans—then next spring I can either reinforce the western coalition against Russia or turn south to contest Austria's Balkan consolidation depending on how the board reshapes.
RUSSIA
I'm at 6 supply centers (MOS, SEV, STP, WAR, NWY, BER) after losing SWE to England's F BAL in Fall 1905—a setback that signals England is now my primary threat in the north. My immediate goal this spring is to consolidate my position and resume growth: I'll push A PRU into Livonia to claim unowned ground and anchor my western flank decisively, advance A WAR into Galicia to threaten Austria's core and force them to defend instead of probing me, hold A STP and A MOS to anchor my rear and support the chain, and maintain F BLA and F SEV dominance in the Black Sea to lock Austria out of the south. Within two turns I aim to reach 8–9 supply centers (Livonia, Galicia, plus my current 6), force Austria and Germany to defend their cores instead of expanding, and position myself to either stabilize the east or pivot west to contain England and France if the board demands it—this is my critical window to break out before the western powers consolidate their lead further.
TURKEY
Russia has fractured under coordinated pressure and now sits at 6 centers after losing Berlin and Prussia to the coalition; I hold 6 centers and must consolidate my Balkan gains while pivoting west into the Mediterranean to exploit Italy's weakness and claim Rome and Naples before England or France can. This spring I'll press A RUM north into UKR to support Austria's continued pressure on Russia's southern flank, hold A SYR defensively to anchor my eastern core, and position my three fleets (F ANK, F SMY, F EAS) to support the Balkan offensive while preparing to break into the Tyrrhenian and Ionian seas next fall to seize Italian supply centers—if Austria holds the Balkans firm and England/France continue pressing Russia from the north and west, Russia will collapse entirely by fall 1906, and I can consolidate the Mediterranean as the board reshuffles around the new power vacuum.
