ITALY
Russia at 7 centers dominates the board and must be contained before it consolidates toward 9-10 this winter—I'm pivoting A MUN west into RUH to support England's pressure on Germany and France's Rhine defense while my fleets hold the Mediterranean secure, positioning myself to either reinforce the western coalition if Russia breaks that way or pivot back east toward the Balkans if Austria signals it needs help against a southern Russian thrust. This fall I'm testing whether the western powers (England, France) will actually pressure Russia's German and northern allies, or whether I need to shift my entire strategy eastward to coordinate with Austria and Turkey against Russia's core before it becomes unstoppable by winter 1905.
RUSSIA

My immediate goal is to push A SIL into Livonia to claim unowned ground and anchor my western flank decisively, advance A WAR into Galicia to threaten Austria's core and force them to defend instead of probing me, hold A MOS and A LVN to support the western advance and anchor my rear, and maintain F BLA and F SEV dominance in the Black Sea to lock Austria out of the south. TURKEY
Russia remains the board's dominant threat at seven centers, and the anti-Russia coalition (Austria, England, France, Italy, and I) has held firm through spring 1905—now I must press the attack decisively in fall to fracture Russia before it consolidates further. I'll move A SER north into RUM to support Austria's Balkan pressure and deny Russia its southern anchor, hold A SYR defensively to secure my eastern core, and position my three fleets (F ANK, F SMY, F EAS) to support the Balkan offensive and maintain control of the straits and eastern Mediterranean. If Austria, England, France, and Italy maintain coordinated pressure through fall 1905 and into winter, Russia will fragment under simultaneous attacks from four directions, and I can consolidate my gains in the Balkans and Mediterranean while pivoting toward 18 centers in the years ahead.
