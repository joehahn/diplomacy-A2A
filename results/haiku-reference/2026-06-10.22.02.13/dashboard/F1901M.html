Initial strategies (pre-negotiation), each power's self-authored plan before talking.
AUSTRIA
I'm in a strong position after S1901M: I hold Serbia (taken as planned), and Russia honored the partition by taking Galicia and Ukraine while avoiding Romania. Now I need to consolidate my Balkan foothold by pushing A SER into Romania or Bulgaria to claim a fourth supply center, while A VIE supports that advance and F TRI remains flexible to defend the Adriatic or pivot south. My immediate goal is to reach 4 supply centers by Winter 1901, establishing myself as a credible Balkan power; from there, I'll watch Russia and Turkey closely—Russia is growing fast (now 5 centers with Galicia), so I need to either deepen my alliance with them by respecting their sphere while I expand west into the Balkans, or begin positioning to check them with Italian or German support if they push too aggressively south. I'll probe Turkey this round to confirm they're still willing to let me take Romania without contest, and I'll signal to Russia that I'm consolidating the south, not threatening them, so they stay focused on the north and west where the real action is.
ENGLAND
I've secured Denmark, Norway, and Sweden as planned, giving me six centers and dominant naval control in the North. My agreements with France, Germany, and Russia have held—each of us expanded into uncontested territory without collision. Now I need to consolidate my position and prepare for the next phase of growth: I'll hold my current line in the North while probing whether to pivot south against France (taking the Low Countries and Channel approaches) or east against Germany (pressing the Baltic and continental approaches). The key is reading which neighbor is weakest or most isolated by the board state, then striking before they can consolidate their own gains.
FRANCE
I've taken Spain and Picardy as planned, establishing a foothold in the south and west. Now I need to consolidate: I'll push my army from Picardy into Belgium to claim a fourth supply center before Germany or England can contest it, move my fleet to support that advance and keep the Channel secure, and position my Spanish army to either reinforce the north or pivot toward Burgundy depending on whether Germany remains trustworthy or has become a threat I need to check. My goal is to reach four supply centers by Winter 1901, then reassess alliances—if Germany is still honoring the non-aggression pact, I'll continue the partnership; if it's moved into Belgium or shown signs of turning, I'll pivot toward England and Italy to gang up on the Rhine and contain German expansion.
GERMANY
I've consolidated the north and center well: F BAL controls the Baltic, A SIL is positioned to take PRU or support deeper pushes, and A MUN can pivot west or east. My immediate goal is to secure PRU (a supply center that denies Russia and opens the eastern front), then push into Belgium and the Rhineland to threaten France before it becomes too strong. Over the next two turns, I want to control at least 5-6 supply centers (adding PRU, BEL, and one of RUH or BUR) to establish myself as a real power while France and Russia are still consolidating. I'll keep my agreements with Russia on Galicia and England on the North Sea, but I'm ready to pivot west and stab France if it overextends into Burgundy or the Rhine.
ITALY
I'm in a strong position: three supply centers held, with armies in Apulia and Tuscany and a fleet in Naples, all positioned to expand further. My immediate goal is to secure Tunis and Greece this fall—moving A APU toward the Mediterranean and A TUS toward Greece while F NAP supports or advances into the Tyrrhenian—to reach five centers by winter and establish Italy as a genuine Mediterranean power that Austria and Turkey must respect. Within two turns, I want to control the central and southern Mediterranean (Tunis, Greece, and ideally one Balkan center like Rumania or Serbia) so that I'm too strong for either neighbor to attack without serious cost, while keeping my options open to pivot north toward France or the Balkans depending on how the board evolves and which powers start to dominate.
RUSSIA
I'm consolidating my gains from spring: A WAR holds Galicia, A UKR pushes south into Rumania to secure the Black Sea approaches and deny Austria a second Balkan center, F RUM moves into the Black Sea proper to anchor my southern flank, and F BOT advances west toward Sweden to claim a northern supply center and threaten Germany's Baltic access. This secures five supply centers (MOS, SEV, STP, WAR, plus RUM or UKR depending on resolution) and positions me to build a fleet or army in winter, while keeping pressure on both Austria and Turkey to honor the partition—if either breaks faith, my fleets control the Black Sea and my armies hold the interior, giving me room to pivot and punish.
TURKEY
I've secured Bulgaria, Armenia, and the eastern Mediterranean as planned. Now I need to consolidate these gains and push toward Syria and Greece while the western powers are distracted. This fall, I'll move A BUL toward Serbia or Greece to claim a second Balkan center, push A ARM into Syria to lock down the eastern coast, and use F BLA to support these advances and maintain naval dominance in the Black Sea. By winter I should control 5–6 supply centers (ANK, CON, SMY, BUL, ARM, SYR) and be positioned to either expand further into the Balkans if Austria weakens, or pivot west toward the Mediterranean if Italy overreaches.
