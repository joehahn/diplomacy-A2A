Initial strategies (pre-negotiation), each power's self-authored plan before talking.
AUSTRIA
Italy took Trieste from me last fall, so my priority this turn is recapturing it: I'll throw F VEN and A VIE at Trieste with A SER and A BUD backing the strike to overwhelm Italy's F TRI. A RUM holds my eastern center and keeps Galicia quiet with Russia while I focus the whole line on retaking Trieste. A turn or two out I want Trieste back, Vienna refilled or pushed forward, and my Balkan wall solid against both Italy and Turkey while France's runaway seven stays the table's real problem.
ENGLAND
I now hold Denmark for six centers and Germany is collapsed at three, so my growth lane is straight into the German Baltic: I want to take Kiel next, pushing F DEN toward Kiel while A SWE and F NTH cover the line. This turn I'll keep my North Sea wall intact, hold Denmark firmly against any German counter, and probe Germany's collapse — Berlin and Kiel are the realistic gains over the next two turns. I keep Russia quiet in the north and the Channel a buffer with France so my whole weight points at the disintegrating German center.
FRANCE
I'm done grinding Belgium and need to actually grow, so this turn I push F MAO to Spain's south coast to take Spain (banking an 8th center) while A MAR supports the convoy/move and A BRE backs the Atlantic. I'll keep A BEL/A HOL/A BUR as a mutually-supporting fortress against any German or English probe and hold F ENG in the Channel as a buffer per my deal with England. A turn or two out I want Spain and Portugal locked, a Winter build at Paris, and either Germany pried into cutting England's North Sea or a clean Channel break west when England's wall thins.
GERMANY
I'm boxed in at three centers with France at seven threatening a solo, so my priority is to break west and grab a fourth center while keeping my Russian and English borders quiet. This turn I'll push A RUH at Burgundy to pin France's western armies and back England's Belgium pressure, while F KIE and F BER hold my Baltic line; if France leaves Holland or Ruhr thin next turn, I swing A RUH in to take it. A turn or two out I want A RUH sitting in Holland or Burgundy with a fleet poised for the North Sea once England and France are fully committed against each other.
ITALY
I finally broke into Trieste last turn — now I consolidate it and grind Austria's collapsing south while Russia's A GAL and Turkey press his eastern flank. This turn I drive F TRI/F ADR-line into the Adriatic and push A ALB/A GRE/F ION onto Serbia, aiming to take Serbia as my sixth center while Austria can't cover Budapest, Serbia, and the Adriatic at once. A turn or two out I want Serbia and the Adriatic firmly mine, then I pivot hard on Turkey before he consolidates behind me, freeing Rome for a Winter fleet build.
RUSSIA
I'm still stuck at 4 centers while Austria sits on my Rumania at 5, so my cleanest growth this turn is to swing F SEV and A GAL together onto RUM: with A GAL S F SEV - RUM and F SEV - RUM I can contest it, though Austria's BUD/SER backing makes that hard alone — so I'll lean on Turkey's A BUL to hit RUM simultaneously and split Austria's defense. In the north I'll keep A STP and A FIN walled with England's quiet front intact, and consider pulling A STP toward MOS later to open a build slot. A turn or two out I want RUM recovered, MOS or WAR open for a Winter build, and Austria pinned between me and Italy.
TURKEY
Austria sits at five centers with Rumania, Serbia, and Trieste lost to Italy's pressure, so this turn I take Greece off Italy and reposition: A BUL into Greece with F AEG convoy/support cracks Italy's southern anchor while I keep CON and ANK covering home. A turn or two out I want Greece in hand, F AEG pressing into the Ionian or Aegean to contest Italy's fleets, and my eastern stack pivoting toward whichever of Italy or Austria is weakest while France runs fat at seven in the west. The real long game is breaking into the central Med before France's seven becomes unstoppable.

AUSTRIA
Everyone is converging on me—Italy holds Trieste, Russia threatens Galicia/Rumania, and Turkey threatens Serbia—so I can't both retake Trieste and defend the east. I'll prioritize keeping my core centers: A BUD and A SER hold to wall Budapest and Serbia, A RUM holds with A SER backing it against the Sevastopol push, A VIE attacks Trieste with F VEN supporting to at least force Italy off my home center, accepting I likely bounce. Going forward I consolidate the Balkan wall and look to peel one attacker—probably offering Russia a quiet Galicia—so I'm not fighting three fronts at once.
ENGLAND
Germany believes my fleets stay off its Baltic, but its coast is collapsed at three centers and Kiel is the clean prize — I'm breaking the quiet-coast deal now. I'll push F DEN into Kiel with A SWE moving to Denmark to backfill and F NTH holding the North Sea line, while F NWY and F EDI anchor the north and F LON covers home. Over the coming turns I press into Berlin and the German Baltic for an eighth and ninth center, keeping Russia quiet and the Channel a buffer with France while I dismantle Germany.
FRANCE
I'm pushing F MAO to Spain's south coast to bank my 8th center, with A MAR supporting that move and A BUR holding firm against Germany's A RUH probe; A BRE backs the Atlantic coast and A BEL/A HOL stay mutually supporting as my northern fortress with F ENG anchoring the Channel buffer per my deal with England. Germany will bounce off BUR and gain nothing, so my border holds without conceding anything; once Spain locks I'll have an Atlantic fleet freeing up to read the Med with Italy and coordinate western pressure on Germany with England before either northern power runs away.
GERMANY
I'm sending A RUH at Burgundy to pin France's western armies as agreed with England, while F KIE and F BER stay home holding my Baltic line and backing each other. France refuses to vacate Holland or RUH this turn, so A RUH will likely bounce, but it keeps France split and keeps my deal with England alive. Next turn I watch whether France actually clears RUH as promised—if so A RUH walks in for my fourth center; if they keep stalling, I'll consider swinging a fleet north against England's overextension once their stack is fully committed west.
ITALY
Austria is putting four units on Trieste, so I'll defend it with F TRI holding plus A ALB supporting the hold, while A GRE holds Greece to keep my center and F ION supports A ALB's defense from the flank, with F NAP backing the Ionian line. I'm not throwing A ALB onto Serbia for Turkey — holding Trieste against Austria's assault is the priority this phase, and I'll only pivot to Serbia once the Adriatic is firmly mine and Austria's wall has cracked from Russia's Galicia pressure. Going forward I consolidate Trieste, lean on Russia and Turkey to keep Austria's east split, and free Rome for a Winter fleet to deepen my Adriatic wedge before Turkey turns on me.
RUSSIA
I'm driving F SEV - RUM with A GAL supporting it, betting Turkey's A BUL cuts A SER at Serbia so Austria's Rumania falls undefended back to me, while keeping A STP and A FIN walled in the quiet north. If Austria genuinely commits A BUD west against Italy as he claims, RUM has no second defender and I reclaim my home center; if it bounces I shift to pressing BUD next Fall with Italy already in Trieste. My near-term aim is to recover Rumania, keep MOS open for a Winter build, and lean on Austria's collapsing south.
TURKEY
I'm throwing A BUL - SER to cut Austria's A SER as agreed with Italy, but since Italy declined to support my entry I'll back it myself with A CON S A BUL - SER so Serbia actually lands in my hands rather than just bouncing. F AEG covers the strait near Greece and A ANK holds the home line at Constantinople's rear, keeping Sevastopol clean for Russia. Next turn I consolidate Serbia, press F AEG into the Aegean approaches against Italy's overstretched fleets, and watch for the moment to pivot my growing eastern stack against whoever emerges strongest from the Austrian collapse. Commentary
  • Austria's Trieste assault completely failed: despite threatening Italy with a four-unit stack in messages, Russia's F SEV dislodged A RUM (cutting its support role), Turkey's A BUL bounced on Serbia rather than supporting Austria, and Italy held Trieste with F TRI backed by A ALB — Austria lost a unit and gained nothing.
  • Russia successfully retook Rumania from Austria by driving F SEV→RUM with A GAL support, exactly as coordinated with Turkey and Italy — Austria's eastern flank is now crumbling on schedule, and Russia climbs back toward relevance.
  • Italy and Turkey's coordination was only partially effective: Turkey threw A BUL at Serbia as promised, but the bounce means Serbia didn't fall — Italy privately asked Turkey to support A BUL→SER with A ALB, but Italy ordered A ALB to support F TRI instead, prioritizing defense of Trieste over delivering Serbia to Turkey.
  • France quietly banked Spain (F MAO→SPA/SC) while keeping every neighbor pacified with reassuring messages — England, Germany, and Italy all received "border frozen" pledges and none moved against France, letting the runaway leader reach 8 centers essentially uncontested.
  • Germany's A RUH→BUR bounced as France openly predicted it would, wasting Germany's only offensive unit while France's A BUR held with A MAR support — Germany threatened to end the quiet border but delivered nothing, and remains stuck at 3 centers with no credible leverage.
  • Austria's retreat of the Rumania army to Ukraine is a bold forward positioning, keeping military pressure on Russia's heartland rather than falling back to safety.
  • Austria signals it has no intention of releasing its grip on the eastern front, with Ukraine now a staging ground that threatens Moscow, Warsaw, and Sevastopol simultaneously.
  • Russia faces a dire squeeze — an Austrian army in Ukraine combined with any Turkish pressure from the south could unravel what remains of the Russian position.
  • The retreat suggests Austria is coordinating with or at least not threatened by Turkey, as moving deeper into Russian territory only makes sense if the southern flank is secure.
