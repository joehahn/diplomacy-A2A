France's whole western stack (RUH, BUR, plus A HOL) is now aimed at retaking Belgium, so my priority this turn is to hold Belgium with maximum support rather than reach for Holland. I'll hold F BEL backed by F NTH and possibly F EDI, keep France's A HOL and RUH bouncing off my walled line, and rely on Germany continuing to pin France's RUH/MUN at Kiel. A turn or two out, once France's stack grinds itself down against my Belgium wall and Germany's Kiel, I swing back to take Holland and press France's coast while my north stays quiet with Russia.
FRANCE
England sits on Belgium with six fleets pinning my coast, so my whole western stack turns on retaking Belgium: A RUH and A BUR both drive Belgium with A HOL supporting the assault from the north, and F ENG threatens Picardy/Belgium while F MAO and A PAR/A MAR guard Brest and the Atlantic. Germany's border stays quiet per our deal—A HOL won't touch Kiel—and I want Belgium banked by Winter for a build, with my fleets free next year to press England's North Sea alongside German support. If I can crack Belgium I'm back to expansion mode against England, the real threat now at six centers.
GERMANY
France pulled RUH off my Kiel line per our deal, so this turn I keep walling Kiel with F BER and F DEN both supporting A KIE, holding my three centers intact while France and England grind each other in Belgium/Holland. With France committed west against England and Russia quiet in Livonia, my real opening over the next turn or two is to throw F DEN at England's North Sea or Sweden once their fleets are tied down, or strike for Munich if France leaves it thin. For now I hold the wall and watch which neighbor overextends first.

This turn I'll attack Serbia with A GRE supported by F ION, push F ADR onto Trieste's flank to threaten A SER's reinforcement, and bring A TUN up by convoy toward the Balkan front while F NAP covers the Ionian. A turn or two out I want Serbia in hand, F ADR pressuring Trieste, and Rome or Venice freed for a Winter build before Turkey (now taking Rumania and growing behind me) gets strong enough to turn on me.
RUSSIA
I lost Rumania to Turkey, so now I consolidate north for my Winter build: A WAR is in Livonia, MOS and WAR are open, and I'll secure a build by keeping STP and the Baltic walled against England while taking either LVN or PRU off Germany's wreck. F SEV stays defensive against Turkey's Black Sea push since I can't retake RUM alone, and I'll keep A FIN/A STP/F BOT anchoring the northern corner per my clean-north deal with England and Germany. A turn or two out I want a build off the open home triangle, the STP corner secure, and to start pressing Germany's thinned Baltic centers or revisit Bulgaria with Austria once Italy clears his Adriatic flank.
TURKEY
My F RUM grab landed Spring — I now hold Rumania, so this Fall I lock it down with F RUM holding while A BUL supports it, and move A ANK to CON to vacate SMY for a Winter build that takes me to five centers. F AEG stays flexible near Greece, supporting the Bulgaria-Rumania anchor or poised to pivot onto Austria's Serbia line next year alongside Italy. A turn or two out I want F AEG and A BUL converging on Serbia as Italy presses Trieste, finishing Austria's south while my Black Sea holdings stay sealed against a collapsing Russia.

  • Italy's coordinated push on Serbia collapsed entirely: Turkey refused to cut A BUD or A SER despite Italy's urgent appeals, A GRE→SER bounced off Austria's intact wall, and F ADR→TRI also bounced — Italy spent the phase stationary on all fronts despite elaborate messaging with both Turkey and Austria.
  • England's northern anchor (NWY, SKA, SWE) held firm and Russia kept its word on the quiet north, but England lost Belgium to France regardless — the Russia-England quiet corridor is real, but it couldn't save England from France's western pressure.
  • England's retreat of F BEL → PIC pushes a fleet deep into French home territory, keeping pressure on France's northern coast despite losing Belgium.
  • France, leading with 7 centers, now faces an English naval presence at Picardy — a direct threat to Paris and Brest that France cannot ignore.
  • England's willingness to retreat forward rather than disband signals they are not conceding the western theater and still intend to contest French dominance.
  • With no messages preceding this phase, the retreat appears uncoordinated with any ally, suggesting England is acting defensively but opportunistically on its own initiative.
