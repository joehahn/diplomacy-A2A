Germany is gutted to BER/KIE, so this turn I take Kiel cleanly (F HOL-KIE supported by A DEN) for my eighth center while keeping NTH/EDI/LON anchoring home, then pivot my whole northern stack toward Belgium and the Channel because France's lead is the only thing that can beat me. A turn or two out I want fleets in NTH/ENG threatening BEL and a foothold on the German Baltic, squeezing France from the north while Russia and Austria keep them busy in the center.
FRANCE
I took Munich last turn and Germany is down to two fleets — this turn I crack Kiel directly with A MUN-KIE backed by A RUH while A BEL holds, finishing Germany's collapse and opening a build. England is now the runaway at 7 fleets and my real threat, so I'll keep F ENG anchoring the Channel, A PAR and A MAR covering my home line, and F SPA/SC banked on Spain while I decide whether to swing it toward Lyon. A turn or two out I want Kiel and Berlin in hand, a fresh build at home, and my Atlantic fleet pivoting south to read the Med against England before that buffer stops paying me.
GERMANY
I'm down to two fleets at Berlin and Kiel, boxed between England's seven-fleet wall and France's seven-unit western stack, with no land army left to retake Munich. My only realistic survival play is to keep F KIE and F BER mutually supporting so neither home center falls, while watching for England and France to finally turn on each other so a gap opens on my coast. A turn or two out I want a fleet probing HEL or DEN if England overcommits west against France, but this turn I hold the Baltic block tight and try to talk England into keeping his fleets off me.
ITALY
I retook Greece last fall and now sit at four centers, but France at seven and England at seven are the real threats while Austria has rebuilt to five and Turkey holds the Aegean. This turn I consolidate my Ionian-Greek line — holding Greece, keeping F ION/F NAP/F ALB cohesive against Turkey's Aegean stack — and probe whether Austria will keep the Adriatic truce so I can angle fleets toward Turkey or pivot through Albania. A turn or two out I want to grind Turkey's Bulgaria/Aegean for a fifth center while keeping Austria off my Adriatic, since stalling at four just lets France and England lap the board.
RUSSIA
Budapest has held at defense-two for three straight turns, so I'm done bleeding against Austria's wall; instead I'll consolidate my home triangle for a Winter build by pulling A STP back toward Moscow while A FIN walls the corner, and keep A GAL/A WAR poised to pivot. The real strategic shift is that France at 7 and England at 7 are the runaway threats, so I want to lean east-defensive against Turkey's growing fleets at the Black Sea with F RUM holding, and look to grab a free center if Austria or Turkey cracks. A turn or two out I want Moscow open for a build, Galicia and Rumania secure, and pressure ready to swing onto Vienna or the Black Sea wherever the line breaks first.
TURKEY
Serbia remains the prize: I'm at four centers and stuck, and Austria's A SER is the last soft Balkan center I can reach. This turn I drive A BUL - SER with A GRE supporting the move — wait, Italy holds Greece now, so my real lever is A BUL - SER backed by A CON, while F AEG and F SMY cover the straits and home. With Russia pinning Budapest from Galicia, Austria can't backstop Serbia; a turn or two out I want Serbia in hand, then pivot my eastern stack against whichever of Russia or Italy overextends as France runs fat at seven in the west.

  • Germany's fleet retreats to Heligoland, a defensive posture suggesting Berlin is bracing for further English or French naval pressure in the North Sea region.
  • With only 2 supply centers, Germany is effectively a dying power — Heligoland is a last stand position, not a springboard for recovery.
  • England and France, tied at 7 centers each, are the dominant forces likely squeezing Germany out; whoever absorbs the German remnants gains a critical edge in their rivalry.
  • The lack of any private messaging before this phase suggests Germany has no active diplomatic lifeline — no power is offering Berlin a meaningful deal.
