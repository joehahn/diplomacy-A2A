Commentary
  • Italy told Austria it would keep A TYR flexible for Lepanto, but actually used it to support Germany's retake of Munich — a clean betrayal of Austria's request to move A TYR to TRI, leaving Trieste undefended and forcing Austria to redirect A BUD there instead.
  • Germany and Italy coordinated effectively: Italy's A TYR supported A SIL → MUN, successfully dislodging France from Munich despite France's warnings to both powers about Russian opportunism in Silesia.
  • Russia walked into Silesia (A WAR → SIL) exactly as France predicted and warned Germany about — Germany ignored the warning, attacked Munich anyway, and now has no unit in SIL to contest Russia's arrival, handing Russia a forward position into Central Europe for free.
  • Austria and Turkey both moved on Bulgaria simultaneously and bounced, wasting both their turns exactly as Turkey warned Austria would happen — Austria ignored Turkey's plea to pull back, and neither power gains the center.
  • England did nothing to pressure France despite Germany's repeated requests to move F BEL toward BUR or RUH — England held all its units, suggesting it is not ready to commit against France and may be keeping options open against both France and Germany simultaneously.
  • France's retreat of the Munich army into Berlin is a bold opportunistic grab, turning a defensive retreat into an offensive strike that puts pressure directly on Germany's home center.
  • Germany, already reduced to 3 supply centers, now faces a French army sitting in its capital — an existential threat that could eliminate Germany from the board entirely by fall.
  • France at 5 centers with a unit in Berlin signals aggressive western expansion; England should take note that a French army this far east changes the balance of power significantly.
  • Whoever holds Germany's alliance — England, Russia, or Austria — now has a decision to make: let France absorb Germany and grow stronger, or intervene to keep Germany alive as a buffer.
