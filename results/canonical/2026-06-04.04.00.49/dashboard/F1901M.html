Commentary
  • France stabbed England on Belgium: England explicitly asked France to stay out of BEL and redirect A BUR elsewhere, France agreed to coordinate — then moved A BUR directly into Belgium anyway, taking the center England considered its natural prize and starting their relationship on the worst possible footing.
  • Turkey successfully seized Romania despite Russia's warnings: Russia threatened a strength-2 defense and offered a last-chance diplomatic deal; Turkey pressed the attack anyway and dislodged F RUM, emerging as the early game's most aggressive and successful power.
  • Germany quietly deceived Russia on Warsaw: Germany told Russia that A SIL was heading to Warsaw while simultaneously assuring Russia of "no hostile intent" — Russia was focused on defending Romania and the bounce in GAL means Germany's WAR push also failed, but the duplicity is clear.
  • Austria and Turkey executed a clean Balkan carve-up: both honored their agreement — Austria took Greece uncontested, Turkey took Romania, and neither interfered with the other, establishing a functional southern entente heading into 1902.
  • England and Germany's North Sea deal held: Germany confirmed it would not contest Belgium and redirected F DEN to SKA rather than SWE — England took Norway cleanly, and Germany took Holland, making this the most reliable bilateral agreement of the fall.
  • Russia's fleet retreats from Rumania to Sevastopol, signaling pressure in the Black Sea region likely from Turkish naval competition — the fight for the south is already heating up.
  • With Russia pulling back from Rumania, Austria or Turkey may have secured or contested that center, leaving Russia's Balkan ambitions temporarily checked.
  • Russia's 5-center standing despite the retreat suggests they compensated elsewhere (likely Scandinavia or Warsaw), keeping pace with England and France at the top of the board.
  • The absence of any pre-phase diplomacy is telling — this retreat was forced and uncoordinated, suggesting Russia was caught off-guard rather than executing a planned repositioning.
  • Germany's 3-center position after Fall 1901 is the most precarious on the board — squeezed between England at 5 and France at 5, Berlin could become the next major flashpoint.
