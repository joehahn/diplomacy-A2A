Commentary
  • France executed exactly what it promised England and Russia — A BUR → MUN supported by A BER — and takes Munich, reaching 7 centers. England stood by watching from NTH rather than pressing HOL as France suggested, meaning France grows while England gains nothing this turn.
  • Russia honored its commitment to Austria, sending F SEV to support A SER → RUM, but the attack bounced because Turkey held A RUM in place with CON support. Austria and Russia coordinated correctly; Turkey's defensive setup was simply stronger than anticipated.
  • Turkey told Russia it was breaking their border deal and pressing A RUM toward SEV, yet Russia still offered to pull support if Turkey recommitted — Turkey ignored this and held RUM defensively rather than advancing. Turkey's stated aggression toward SEV was a bluff; the real goal was simply surviving the Austrian-Russian pincer.
  • Italy and Austria verbally confirmed a joint squeeze on Turkey (F GRE → BUL/SC, A SER → RUM), but Italy's push into BUL/SC also bounced against Turkey's hold, leaving both Austria and Italy empty-handed while Turkey successfully defended on two fronts simultaneously.
  • Germany's A HOL → KIE recaptures a home center and A MUN → BUR successfully bounced France's advance, but MUN is still dislodged by France's supported attack — Germany's counter-strategy delayed France by one unit but couldn't prevent the center loss, leaving Germany at 3 and critically endangered.
  • Germany's retreat of the Munich army to Ruhr signals a collapsing German center, with the core heartland now exposed and vulnerable to both French and Austrian pressure.
  • With only 3 supply centers and a retreating army pushed westward, Germany is effectively on life support — any power with units near the Rhine or Bavaria can deliver a killing blow.
  • The Ruhr position suggests Germany may be angling to hold against France rather than contest the east, implying a tacit acceptance that whoever drove into Munich (likely France or Austria) has effectively won that front.
