Commentary
  • Austria and Italy executed a clean coordinated attack on Greece: Austria supported Italy's F AEG → GRE with F ALB as agreed, dislodging Turkey's F GRE — despite Austria's late messages trying to redirect Italy into supporting *Austria's* A BUL → GRE instead. Italy got the center, not Austria.
  • England and France followed through on their coordinated plan against Germany: England dislodged F HEL with F NTH supported by F DEN, while France took the undefended HOL with A RUH — Germany loses two units in one turn exactly as messaged, confirming this Anglo-French cooperation is real and functional.
  • Germany's desperate last-minute gambit — moving F HOL → BEL to punish France for vacating it — succeeded in reaching BEL, but Germany simultaneously lost F HEL, leaving them in a net-zero or worse position and likely facing further collapse next fall.
  • Russia's F SEV → BLA bounced off Turkey's F BLA, meaning Russia failed to neutralize Turkey's support mechanism — but Turkey's F SMY → CON attack also failed because Russia held F CON with F RUM support, so the mutual threat cancelled out and the southern front stagnated for Russia.
  • Turkey told Austria it had confirmed a mutual hold on GRE/BUL, but Austria never honored it — Austria supported Italy into GRE, betraying the standoff Turkey thought it had secured, and Turkey's F GRE is now dislodged with nowhere clean to retreat.
  • Germany's fleet retreating to Kiel signals continued pressure on the German North Sea position, keeping England and Russia as the dominant naval threats in that theater.
  • Turkey pulling the Greek fleet back to the Ionian Sea preserves a defensive naval anchor in the eastern Mediterranean, suggesting Turkey is bracing for further Austrian or Italian encroachment rather than mounting any counteroffensive.
  • Russia's 7-center lead makes it the power to watch — both Germany and Turkey are retreating away from Russian pressure, and neither appears capable of meaningful resistance without outside help.
  • France at 6 centers remains the only power with the heft to potentially challenge Russia, but the absence of any coordination messaging is telling — an alliance of convenience may be forming by necessity rather than design.
  • Austria at 5 centers is quietly well-positioned between a retreating Turkey and a distracted Russia, and the Ionian retreat by Turkey opens the door for Austrian naval ambitions in the Aegean.
