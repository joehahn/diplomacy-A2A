Commentary
  • France successfully coordinated with Russia to take HOL: A BEL advanced with A KIE support, dislodging England's F HOL — a clean Franco-Russian squeeze that England failed to prevent despite repeated diplomatic warnings to both powers.
  • England's attempt to retake KIE collapsed entirely: F HOL was dislodged before it could even contest KIE, and Russia held firm with Baltic support — England's 9-center lead is now under genuine structural pressure from the west and north simultaneously.
  • Turkey told Austria it would redirect A CON toward BUL rather than SER, and Austria moved A BUD to SER expecting a bounce — Turkey kept its word, but Austria's SER grab now looks opportunistic rather than defensive, and leaves BUD unoccupied and vulnerable.
  • Italy attacked TRI again despite Austria explicitly warning it would be supported to strength 2 — the bounce confirms Austria's defensive hold is solid, but Italy is burning tempo while Turkey quietly secures BUL and ANK repositions toward SMY for builds.
  • Germany's A BOH → MUN was bounced by Russia's A SIL pushing simultaneously — Russia told Germany it wasn't targeting MUN while coordinating pressure that denied it anyway, effectively eliminating Germany's only meaningful move without formally committing to the kill.
  • England's retreat of the Holland fleet to Heligoland keeps a naval presence in the German Bight, maintaining pressure on the North Sea approaches and signaling continued interest in finishing off Germany's last hold.
  • With England consolidating in Heligoland, France should be watching carefully — an 8-center England with a strong North Sea position is only a few good moves from a runaway lead.
  • The retreat is tactically clean for England; Heligoland is a defensible position that threatens both Kiel and the broader German coast, accelerating Germany's likely elimination.
  • Russia and Turkey, both at 6 and 5 centers respectively, need to be aware that England's western consolidation frees London to turn its diplomatic attention — and eventually its fleets — toward the east.
