Commentary
  • England executed a clean two-fleet grab of Holland, stripping Germany of a home center despite sending Germany vague cooperation signals right up to orders close — England was never seriously entertaining a deal.
  • Russia declined to support Austria's BUL attack with F RUM, instead marching A WAR→PRU and leaving Austria's two-unit push to bounce off Turkey's defended BUL — Austria's coordinated squeeze on Turkey collapsed entirely because Russia prioritized western expansion over the agreed Balkan pincer.
  • Italy defied Austria's explicit warning by moving F ION→GRE, but the move bounced — Austria had already vacated GRE with F GRE→BUL/SC, meaning both powers wasted their moves and Turkey's BUL held; Italy's "deal" with Austria was hollow on both sides.
  • France and Italy appeared genuinely coordinated on MUN — Italy confirmed A TYR support and France committed A BUR→MUN — but Germany's BOH support held MUN, turning the joint Franco-Italian push into a costly bounce that leaves both powers no closer to cracking Germany.
  • Turkey is quietly consolidating: A BUL held with F BLA support, F CON pushed into AEG, and Russia never attacked RUM — Turkey successfully played Austria and Russia against each other through contradictory promises, surviving the spring intact while gaining Aegean reach toward Greece and the eastern Mediterranean.
  • Germany's retreat of the Holland army to Ruhr keeps it centrally positioned, suggesting Berlin is preparing to contest the western front rather than ceding ground quietly.
