Commentary
  • England and Germany executed a clean coordinated deal: England supported A RUH → HOL as promised, Germany moved there as agreed, and France's A BEL was left holding an empty bag — France told Germany it would support the HOL move but instead moved A BEL toward HOL to contest it, a transparent double-cross that failed regardless.
  • France attempted to play both sides on HOL — promising Germany support for A RUH → HOL while simultaneously telling Russia it was pressing A MUN → KIE and moving A BEL → HOL — and ended up with nothing, bounced out of HOL and failing to take KIE, a strategic disaster of its own making.
  • Russia's A BER → KIE attack bounced exactly as England predicted, with F DEN supporting F KIE at strength 2 vs. strength 2; Russia's gambit to extort STP failed completely, and A WAR → LVN now puts pressure on England's STP — the only successful Russian move this phase.
  • Italy ignored Austria's ceasefire plea and Austria's own diplomatic overtures to Turkey, instead pressing A TYR → VIE with A TRI support and successfully taking Vienna — Austria is now cornered at BUD and SER with Italy threatening both, while Turkey honored the Austria standoff and held A BUL, effectively leaving Austria to die slowly to Italy rather than a two-front collapse.
  • Turkey honored its ceasefire with Russia (A ARM held, F SEV held) and redirected F GRE → AEG to counter Italy's F AEG, but both fleets bounced — Turkey stabilized its Russian flank as promised but failed to neutralize Italy's naval threat to CON.
  • Austria's lone army retreats to Bohemia, a desperate bid to stay relevant with just 2 centers — survival mode, not strategy.
  • Bohemia positions Austria to potentially contest German or Russian territory, but with only 2 centers, any counterattack will be nearly impossible to sustain.
  • England's 8-center lead makes it the clear frontrunner, and every other major power should be eyeing a coalition to curb its dominance before it's too late.
  • The France-Italy-Turkey cluster at 6-7 centers each suggests a competitive mid-tier that will likely turn on one another as England's threat looms — whoever blinks first loses ground to London.
  • Germany's single center is effectively a death rattle; the only question is who absorbs it and gains the tempo advantage heading into 1911.
