Commentary
  • Austria and Russia executed their coordinated BUL attack flawlessly — Russia delivered the promised F RUM support, Austria sent both A SER and F GRE at BUL, and Turkey's A BUL is dislodged. The Austria-Russia partnership is the most effective alliance on the board right now.
  • Turkey's gamble to bluff Austria into leaving BUL open backfired catastrophically: Turkey told Austria "A BUL is moving to RUM, walk in for free," but then actually ordered A BUL → RUM anyway — the result is Turkey loses BUL in a contested fight AND fails to take RUM, leaving Turkey with a dislodged unit and no gains.
  • Italy told France "confirmed: A TYR S A BUR → MUN" and delivered exactly that — but Germany's A BOH support held MUN anyway, so the France-Italy coordination against MUN produced a bounce for the second consecutive fall. France is burning moves on a defended target.
  • Russia grabbed A PRU → BER while Germany's attention was split westward toward HOL and DEN — Germany had explicitly warned Russia this would mean war, but Russia pressed anyway, likely picking up BER as a new center and directly threatening German survival.
  • England is consolidating rather than pressing: F BEL supports F HOL, all other units hold — England is locking in gains and letting France and Russia do the aggressive work, a posture that keeps England at 6 centers while others bleed for new ones.
  • Turkey's retreat of the Bulgarian army to Constantinople signals a significant weakening of Ottoman power in the Balkans, ceding ground likely to Austria or Russia.
  • With only 3 supply centers, Turkey is now on the defensive and Constantinople itself becomes a critical last-stand position — losing it would be existential.
  • Austria at 6 centers appears to be the primary beneficiary of Turkey's Balkan collapse, likely consolidating control over Bulgarian territory.
  • Russia at 6 centers is well-positioned to press Turkey from the north, and the CON retreat suggests the Black Sea/Rumanian front may be heating up against Ottoman interests.
  • England's dominant 7-center position in the west contrasts sharply with the eastern powers' grinding conflict — England may be poised to dictate the endgame while Austria and Russia exhaust themselves finishing off Turkey.
