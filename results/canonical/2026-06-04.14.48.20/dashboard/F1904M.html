Commentary
  • Italy delivered on its promise to Turkey: F ION supported F AEG → GRE, successfully taking the center from Austria despite Austria's earlier confidence that a supported hold would survive — Austria's F GRE was left defending CON, not GRE, exactly as Turkey's final message predicted.
  • Austria was outmaneuvered by Turkey's last-minute pivot: Austria committed F GRE to support A CON against an expected CON attack, but Turkey switched to GRE at the last moment, costing Austria the center and exposing the fragility of Austria's two-front hold.
  • England executed cleanly on its agreement with Russia, taking KIE while Russia pushed A BER → MUN — but both bounced on MUN (France also tried), and Russia's F SWE → BAL displaced Germany's F KIE, meaning England and Russia coordinated but neither got the full payoff they planned.
  • France and England were secretly working at cross-purposes on MUN: England explicitly warned France that Russia was also heading to MUN and suggested France go to RUH instead, but France pushed A BUR → MUN anyway, creating a three-way bounce that let Germany's A MUN survive.
  • Austria told Russia it was moving A GAL → SIL to cut support for BER as a cooperative gesture — and actually did it — but Russia's A SIL was supporting A BER → MUN, not A BER itself, so the cut was largely irrelevant; Austria's "coordination" with Russia helped neither party meaningfully.
  • Austria's retreat of F GRE to BUL/SC keeps a naval presence in the Balkans, signaling continued pressure on Turkey's remaining holdings and resistance to any Russian push toward the Aegean.
  • Germany's retreat of F KIE to HEL is a desperate rearguard move — clinging to the Helgoland Bight with only 2 centers, Germany is barely surviving and likely dependent on a lifeline from England or another power to avoid elimination.
  • With England at 8 centers, the retreat phase underscores that no one has meaningfully checked the English juggernaut — Germany's collapse in the north has effectively gifted England dominance of the seas.
  • Austria at 6 and Russia at 6 remain locked in a tense balance in the east; Austria's Balkan positioning suggests it is not ceding ground to Russia despite any potential cooperation against Turkey.
  • Turkey at 3 centers is in serious danger of elimination, squeezed between Austria's naval retreat consolidating in BUL/SC and whatever pressure Russia is applying from the north.
