Commentary
  • Italy betrayed Turkey's coordination plan: Turkey needed F AEG to move to BUL/SC to cut Austria's support, but Italy instead ordered F AEG → BUL/SC as a *bounce* attempt for itself — the result was that Austria's F BUL/SC support was never cut, Austria's A SMY → CON succeeded, and Turkey's A CON → BUL was dislodged, the opposite of what the two powers agreed to achieve together.
  • Austria outmaneuvered Turkey diplomatically and militarily: Austria told Italy that F AEG would not support Turkey (and Italy confirmed this), then executed A SMY → CON with F BUL/SC support at strength 2, dislodging Turkey's A CON — exactly the attack Austria telegraphed, and Turkey's warnings to Austria that Italy would cut the support proved false.
  • Germany successfully struck England: despite Austria promising to send A GAL → SIL to cut Russia's support for BER (which Austria did not do — A GAL bounced in SIL), Germany pivoted entirely and took KIE with A MUN supported by F BAL, stripping England of a home center and reaching 3 centers rather than being eliminated.
  • France ignored Germany's plea and took MUN anyway, eliminating Germany as a meaningful buffer: Germany explicitly begged France to leave MUN so a living Germany could pressure England from the north, but France marched A BUR → MUN regardless, gutting Germany's last leverage.
  • England held completely still across six units while France grabbed MUN and Germany grabbed KIE — England's passive fall means it failed to grow despite being at 8 centers, and the gap between England and the chasing pack narrows slightly heading into the winter count.
  • England's retreat of the Kiel fleet to Holland consolidates its North Sea dominance, keeping pressure on both Germany's last holdings and France's northern flank.
  • Germany, reduced to 1 center, is effectively on life support — England's repositioning in Holland signals it is positioning to finish the job rather than let anyone else claim the scraps.
  • France should be watching Holland nervously; an English fleet there is a dagger pointed at Brest and the Channel, suggesting the Anglo-French relationship may be approaching a breaking point.
  • Russia and Austria will note that England at 8 centers is pulling away from the pack — if the mid-tier powers don't coordinate against English growth soon, the window to stop a runaway leader is closing fast.
